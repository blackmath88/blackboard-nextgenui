Beyond the scroll

One conversation about AI interfaces, kept as four explainers and a build plan. Each piece answers one question and hands the next one on.

The problem

Chat became the default interface for working with language models. It is good for curiosity and bad for ongoing work. State disappears. Nothing is noticed unless you ask. Every answer is a new entry in the scroll.

In October 2026 OpenAI shipped Intelligent UI: interfaces generated per answer. That raised the question this collection follows. Who decides the form of an interface, where does its state live, and who holds authority over it?

What this is

Four explainers and one build plan, made in a single conversation between a human (Miro, blackmath88) and Claude. Each explainer answers one question and ends with the next one open.

Method

Look back first
Every problem is traced to an older domain that already solved part of it: speech recognition, infrastructure, hypertext, book history, IDEs.
Explain, then simulate
Each explainer is deterministic and runs offline. Simulations are labelled as such.
Evidence labels
Claims carry their source type: announcement, third-party analysis, depends on host, or our design.
End open
Each piece hands its unsolved problem to the next.
Plan in chat, build elsewhere
The conversation produced concepts and explainers. Real builds go to a coding agent.

Meta

This repository is an example of its own subject: a conversation that outgrew chat and had to become a structured place. What you are reading is the workbench the scroll turned into.

Each piece was co-authored with an AI and reviewed by a human. That authorship is stated here rather than hidden.

The conversation kept its own apparatus: a session summary in JSON, with findings, positions taken, prior art, open questions and sources.

Thesis

Language may become the universal input. It does not have to become the universal interface.
